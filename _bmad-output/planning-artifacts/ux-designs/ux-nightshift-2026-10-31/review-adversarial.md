# Adversarial review: Nightshift UX spines (EXPERIENCE.md, DESIGN.md drafts)

Reviewed 2026-11-03 against `.memlog.md` (later entries win), `.working/open-items-2026-11-03.md`, PRD `prd.md` (FR-11–FR-16, FR-20–FR-24, FR-27, FR-28, FR-32, FR-34, FR-47, FR-50–FR-52, PRIV-2–PRIV-9, SEC-1, SEC-7) and `addendum.md` ("Approval surface"). Pending PRD changes (open-items section 2) and the corrected FR-51 are taken as decided. Items marked [OPEN: architecture] are only raised where the UX behaviour around them is itself unsafe.

## Verdict

**Not ready to hand off.** The core rules are right and stated clearly: one repair per approval, action and target from structured data, no approval in Slack or by phone, silence never grants permission, never a stale green, facts separate from the explanation. The weak spots are at the edges, where real nights go wrong: evidence that changes while someone is reading, several people deciding on one request, manual work between "approved" and "I've done it", and mode or channel changes while requests are open. In each case a screen can currently imply that something is approved, reviewed, finished or healthy when it isn't. Two findings are critical: an approval can be committed on evidence other than what the person read, and the console's single approve button can merge the two independent approvals into one click by Orvex.

Counts: 2 critical · 14 high · 13 medium · 6 low.

## Findings

### Critical

- **[critical]** An approval can be committed on evidence the person didn't read, or on stale evidence (Interaction Primitives "Live data"; State Patterns › Surface states "Request re-issued", "Decision not recorded"; Approval validity "always shows current evidence"; Authorisation & approvals "tied to the exact … evidence shown"). *Scenario:* Cris opens B3 at 02:13 and his phone locks. At 02:20 Nightshift re-issues the request: memory is climbing faster and the replica count has changed. He unlocks at 02:31. Mobile browsers suspend background timers, so the tab still shows the 02:13 render, and he taps **Approve this repair**. Variants: an automatic refresh swaps sections 3–7 just as his thumb lands; "try again" after a network error resends the old decision; Josh approves in A3 while the cluster has been out of date since 02:10. The spines say an approval is tied to the evidence shown, but nothing says the tap carries the version that was on screen. Nothing says what the screen does when that version is out of date, or whether Approve is offered on an out-of-date cluster at all. Only A5 shows "Blocked", afterwards. *Fix:* give each request version an ID and have the decision carry the version that was rendered. The server refuses a mismatch, and the screen says "This request changed at 02:20 while you were reading. Nothing was approved. Review the new version.", with the changed sections marked. Decision screens never replace sections 1–8 in place; a banner announces new evidence instead. Approve stays unavailable until every section has loaded, and while the cluster is out of date ("You can't approve this now: no data from shop-eu-1 since 02:10"). "Try again" re-reads the request before resending. A second tap never creates a second decision. Add this failure path to UJ-1 and UJ-3.

- **[critical]** In A3 one button can stand for either of two approvals, so the two-key rule can collapse into one click by Orvex (Information Architecture › A3 "'Approved on behalf of the customer' (delegation)" and the Orvex support row; Voice and Tone › Exact labels "Approval buttons"; Authorisation & approvals table). *Scenario:* the customer has delegated risky repairs to Orvex support. An unticked risky repair needs both the customer's approval and Orvex technical approval. In the same console, someone with support and engineer rights (or two colleagues side by side) opens A3 and clicks the one **Approve this repair** button. The screen doesn't say which approval the click gives. It could be recorded as both, or as the delegated one followed by a technical one from the same person. "Two different people" is [OPEN: architecture], but a single, undifferentiated button makes the unsafe result the easy one. *Fix:* separate actions labelled with the role they exercise: **Give Orvex technical approval** and **Approve on Customer C-0142's behalf (delegated)**. Never offer both to the same person on the same request. Afterwards A3 says "You gave Orvex technical approval at 02:14. The customer's approval must come from someone else." "Approvals still required" names each approval and its person, and the history keeps them as two rows.

### High

- **[high]** A manual repair has no "work started" state, so nobody (and no automatic repair) knows a person is working on the target (Component Patterns › Progress steps "Manual repair needed → Verifying"; Key Flows › Flow 7 steps 5–6). *Scenario:* at 01:10 Marc runs the pre-start check and starts moving traffic to the standby interface. Cris and Josh still see violet "Manual repair needed", which reads as waiting. A ticked safe repair or a policy repair on node n-7 fires halfway through Marc's work, because the repair coordinator (FR-29) doesn't know a person holds the target. In B4 Cris sees "waiting" and assumes nobody has started. *Fix:* after a passing pre-start check, the performer presses **I'm starting now**. This records the start, registers the target with the coordinator and holds conflicting automatic repairs on that target. Status: "Manual repair in progress: Marc started 01:10". **I've done it: check now** appears only after a start.

- **[high]** A window that ends mid-work, and a pre-start check that fails or can't decide, are not designed (State Patterns › Approval validity "Manual repairs"; Surface states "Manual repair, outside the window"; Flow 7 failure line). *Scenario:* Marc starts at 02:50 and the work runs to 03:15. At 03:00 "outside it they are asked again" fires, so Cris is called at 03:00 to re-approve work that is already half-done, or **I've done it** is refused. Separately, the pre-start check fails, or can't run because the cluster is out of date. Nothing stops a person from carrying on, and the spines only say "he doesn't start [ASSUMPTION]". *Fix:* the window governs the start. Work that started inside it may finish; an overrun shows "Running past the approved window (ended 03:00)" and is reported to the approvers, without asking them to approve again. The check result gates **I'm starting now**. On Fail or "Can't tell" the screen says "Don't start: the evidence has changed. Your approvals no longer cover this; the approvers are being asked again." The approvals are cancelled and recorded, and any later "done" report is recorded as work done outside approval.

- **[high]** The history states a self-report as fact and makes up a time span (Key Flows › Flow 7 step 8 "performed by Marc (Orvex) 01:10–01:22 · checked by Nightshift"; State Patterns › Repair status → FR-34 outcome). *Scenario:* Marc presses **I've done it** without doing the steps. The network card recovers on its own, Nightshift's check passes, and B4 and A5 say "performed by Marc 01:10–01:22 · Succeeded" for good. 01:10 was the pre-start check, not the start. A customer looking into a later outage trusts the record. *Fix:* record only what is known: "Pre-start check passed 01:10 · Marc reported it done at 01:22 · Nightshift's check at 01:25: problem gone". Use FR-34's own words, "manual repair reported done". Show a start time only if a start was recorded (previous finding). Say that the check confirms the symptom is gone, not that the steps were followed.

- **[high]** "Succeeded" on a workaround hides a hardware fault that is still open (Key Flows › Flow 7 steps 1 and 7; State Patterns › Incident status; FR-20, FR-52). *Scenario:* Flow 7 ends "the connection is stable on the standby interface: Succeeded", and the incident goes to Resolved (green). The network card is still failing, so nobody replaces it, and next week the standby fails with no spare. The flow also has an Orvex engineer performing what looks like hardware work, while FR-52 gives hardware to the customer or their provider. *Fix:* add an incident state "Worked around: hardware fault still open", not green, with the next step in words: "Ask your hardware provider to replace the network card on n-7. Here's the evidence to send them." Make Flow 7 explicitly an OMK network-configuration workaround done by Orvex. The fault closes only after the customer reports the hardware fixed and Nightshift has checked (FR-20).

- **[high]** The recovery plan reads as already reviewed when the customer approves, and a review that changes the plan doesn't ask the customer again (Component Patterns › Manual-repair panel item 8 "Reviewed by Orvex before the work starts"; Key Flows › Flow 7 steps 2–4). *Scenario:* Cris approves at 23:58. At 00:20 Josh's review changes the plan: n-7 must be drained first, so the interruption is longer. Cris approved the old plan and nothing asks him again. At midnight the past tense "Reviewed by Orvex" reads as done. *Fix:* show the review state literally: "Not reviewed yet: Orvex reviews this before work starts", then "Reviewed by Josh (Orvex) at 00:20". If the review changes the steps, plan, interruption or window, that is a material change (FR-22): the customer's approval is cancelled and they are asked again. Consider asking for Orvex's review before asking the customer.

- **[high]** The "If you don't answer" line hides a last-resort run (Voice and Tone › Exact labels "No answer (customer)"; Component Patterns › Approval card item 9; Authorisation & approvals "Last-resort condition"). *Scenario:* Samira ticked the last-resort condition for "Restart the only replica". A request arrives at 02:13 with failure predicted around 02:40. B3 tells her "we keep watching and change nothing that isn't already authorised". She reads that as "nothing will happen" and leaves it for the morning, or means to reject and is too late. *Fix:* when the last-resort condition covers the repair, item 9 says so: "If nobody answers and checkout is predicted to fail within about 30 minutes, this restart will run on its own (you ticked the last-resort condition on 12 Oct). Reject now if you don't want that." Show the earliest time it could run, and the same in A3.

- **[high]** Customer cards keep healthy words on out-of-date data (Information Architecture › B1 'impact in words ("Checkout works normally")'; State Patterns › Freshness "Customer card: Out of date · Last updated 02:10"; Component Patterns › Stale panel "Does not hide the last known values"). *Scenario:* the cluster stops reporting at 02:10 just as checkout starts failing. At 02:40 B1 still says "Checkout works normally" in large type, next to a small "Out of date" tag. A non-expert reads the sentence, not the tag. The console replaces impact with "Unknown"; the customer view doesn't. *Fix:* on an out-of-date card the impact line becomes "We don't know how checkout is doing: no data since 02:10". The last known value moves to a secondary line ("Last known at 02:10: checkout working"). Apply the same rule to B2 and B5 ("still watching").

- **[high]** Alert-only mode is invisible in the console, and switching mode with requests open is undefined (State Patterns › Surface states "Alert-only mode"; Authorisation & approvals "Alert-only mode"; Information Architecture › A1–A3). *Scenario:* at 02:10 the customer switches to alert-only. At 02:14 Josh approves a platform-only repair for that cluster in A3, because nothing on A1, A2 or A3 shows the mode. The executor refuses it (SEC-7), so Josh sees an unexplained "Blocked" or believes the platform is repaired. The other way round: when the customer switches back to approval-enabled, do approvals given before the switch revive? Do requests opened before alert-only still show **Approve**? *Fix:* show the mode on A1 rows, the A2 header and A3 ("Customer in alert-only mode: Nightshift won't run this. Recommend only."), and don't offer Approve there. On a switch to alert-only, open requests become recommendations and given-but-unused approvals are cancelled, recorded and reported to the approvers. Switching back revives nothing. B6's mode confirmation lists what will happen to the open items, and ticks show "Paused: alert-only mode".

- **[high]** Turning a channel off leaves requests in flight in an undefined state (Privacy & outbound channels table; State Patterns › Surface states "Portal not permitted"; Key Flows › Flow 6 failure line; Authorisation & approvals "Delegation"). *Scenario:* at 02:16 an administrator unticks portal access. Cris's approval is waiting for Orvex and two requests are open: are they cancelled, kept or merely invisible? Slack still sends "Open in Quornex portal to review" to a portal with nothing to show. With notifications off and delegation on, "You're notified of every approval made on your behalf" can't be honoured. SEC-7's "changes are notified" has no channel either. *Fix:* before an untick takes effect, show its consequences with counts: "2 open requests will be withdrawn. Cris's approval from 02:14 will be cancelled. Orvex support can no longer approve on your behalf, because you couldn't be told." Notifications offer the portal link only while the portal is permitted. Delegation needs a working notification route and is suspended, in words, without one. State where notices of settings changes go once notifications are off.

- **[high]** Reject commits on one tap and can't be undone for this occurrence (Component Patterns › Secondary button; Voice and Tone › Exact labels "Reject result"; FR-50). *Scenario:* at 03:00 Cris scrolls to the end and his thumb lands on the full-width **Reject**. The result screen tells him the repair is blocked and that Nightshift won't ask again unless the problem becomes critical or changes. Checkout fails two hours later and he had no way to say yes in between. *Fix:* reopening at the person's own request is not automatic resubmission. The Reject result offers "I rejected this by mistake: show me the request again", valid for this occurrence while the evidence still holds, and recorded. Alternatively, add a short confirmation for Reject on phones. Either way, fix the order of the two buttons and keep space between them.

- **[high]** Conflicting decisions by different people are undefined (Component Patterns › Approval card item 8; Voice and Tone › Exact labels "After Approve" and "Reject result"; Authorisation & approvals "Delegation"). *Scenario:* (a) Cris approves and Josh then rejects. Cris's B3 still says "Waiting for Orvex technical approval", so he goes back to sleep expecting a repair. (b) Orvex support approves on the customer's behalf at 01:03; at 01:05 Cris opens the same request and taps Reject. Which decision wins? (c) Two customer administrators: Samira approves on desktop while Cris rejects on his phone. *Fix:* every approval screen shows each decision already made, with name and time, and tells everyone who acted: "Josh (Orvex) rejected this at 02:20. The repair won't run." Write the rule down: a rejection by any required approver blocks the repair, and the customer's own rejection overrides a delegated approval that hasn't been used yet. A second person sees the first decision and can't submit a decision based on an older screen (first critical finding).

- **[high]** Delegated approvals hide who gave them (Voice and Tone › Exact labels History "Approved by Orvex support on your behalf at 01:03"; FR-34 "by whom"). *Scenario:* an Orvex support agent approves a risky restart that causes an outage. The customer's history says only "Orvex support", so they can't tell who it was or challenge it. Ticks are just as anonymous ("Ticked in your settings on 12 Oct"). *Fix:* "Approved on your behalf by Dana K. (Orvex support) at 01:03 · delegation set by Samira on 20 Oct · scope: risky repairs, all clusters". Name the person on ticks too: "Ticked by Samira on 12 Oct".

- **[high]** AI text can fill the most-read, unlabelled lines (Component Patterns › Approval card items 1–2 and Explanation block; Information Architecture › B2 "FR-11 message first", B1 "impact in words", Slack "one-sentence risk"; SEC-1). *Scenario:* a crafted log line steers the model. The lead sentence at the top of B3 says "Routine maintenance, no impact expected", or the B1 card says "Checkout works normally". The rule keeps only action, target and steps out of AI text. The problem sentence, the impact line, the Slack sentence and the B5 summary sentences have no stated source and no label, and they sit above or outside the yellow block. *Fix:* state the source of every field on B1, B2, B3, B3-M, B5 and the Slack message. Render items 1–2 and the card impact lines from templates over structured fields (impact, time to impact). AI wording appears only inside the labelled yellow block. List the fields that must come from structured data: risk, interruption, recovery plan, validity, approvals still required, What this changes, performer and window.

- **[high]** The fleet button says "300" but means something else, and the set can change under the click (Voice and Tone › Exact labels "Approve rollback for these 300 clusters"; Key Flows › UJ-5 steps 3–6; State Patterns › Freshness "12 clusters can't be included"). *Scenario:* 300 clusters are affected and "12 can't be included", yet the wave totals add up to 300, and 112 of those still need their customer. Josh reads "these 300" and believes all 300 will roll back. The 12 clusters with Fleet reports off may still be on v2.4, and nobody acts or tells those customers. A cluster that joined the group after he opened A4, or one grouped by mistake, rides on his click, and he can't take one out. *Fix:* the button names the exact set it covers when rendered, and the click is refused if the set has changed ("3 clusters joined since you opened this. Review them."). Split the summary: "288 clusters in this approval · 112 also need their customer · 12 not included (Fleet reports off): their customers are told separately". Allow excluding a single cluster. In Step 1, list the exceptions (Fail, out of date, no permission) first instead of a 300-row table.

### Medium

- **[medium]** The Orvex-only info card is static and absolute (Voice and Tone › Exact labels "Orvex-only info card"; Component Patterns › Info card; Authorisation & approvals table). *Scenario:* the card says "Orvex is repairing … No effect on your service expected. No action needed." But the repair is only waiting for Orvex approval, or it fails, or the pre-run check reclassifies it as affecting production (FR-21) and it becomes the customer's request. The card doesn't change. It has no time, status, outcome, link or route to Dispute. In alert-only mode it is simply untrue. *Fix:* the card carries the repair status, time and freshness, and links to a read-only detail page with "What this changes" and **Dispute**. It turns into a request card when the repair is reclassified and closes into B4 and B5 with its outcome. It is never shown as an ongoing repair while the customer is in alert-only mode.

- **[medium]** "Heartbeat OK" on a tile reads as healthy (Component Patterns › Health-grid tile; State Patterns › Freshness "Fleet reports off"). *Scenario:* at 3 AM Josh sees "Heartbeat OK" on 12 tiles and moves on. Orvex can't see problems on those clusters, and the grid may count them as "No open problem". *Fix:* these tiles never show "No open problem". Label them "Agent running · problems not reported to Orvex (customer's choice)", keep them out of the "No open problem" count and use neutral styling.

- **[medium]** "Repairs stopped on this cluster" (FR-28) never shows outside the history row (Voice and Tone › Exact labels "Recovery plan"; Key Flows › UJ-2 failure line). *Scenario:* after a harmful repair the customer assumes their ticked repairs still protect them. No state on B1 or A1 shows the pause, and no surface or person resumes it. *Fix:* add a cluster banner on B1, B2 and A1 (tile and queue): "Automatic repairs stopped on shop-eu-1 since 21:40 after a repair made things worse. Ticked repairs won't run until Orvex resumes them." Add a recorded **Resume** action in the console.

- **[medium]** The reach of a rejection over the same problem is ambiguous (Voice and Tone › Exact labels "Reject result": "Other automatic repairs keep working", "swap in another repair to get around your decision"). *Scenario:* Cris rejects the restart. A ticked safe repair for the same problem (move traffic) then runs, or FR-27 proposes the next repair. Cris either reads "nothing will be done about this" or expects his ticks to stop. *Fix:* the result names them: "Repairs you've ticked for this problem may still run: Move traffic to a healthy copy (ticked 12 Oct). Nothing else will be tried for this problem without asking you."

- **[medium]** The green "Safe" chip contradicts "green means verified", and the grey "Blocked" chip looks calm (DESIGN.md › Colors "Rules that carry the meaning"; `risk-level-chip.safe` → `chip-success`; `repair-status-chip.blocked` → neutral). *Scenario:* on B3 at 3 AM, a green chip beside the repair reads as checked or approved. On a critical problem, a grey "Blocked" chip reads as settled while nothing is being repaired. *Fix:* give Safe a non-green treatment, keeping green for verified, succeeded and resolved. When a repair is Blocked and its problem is still open, B1, B2 and A1 add "Nothing is being repaired".

- **[medium]** There is no customer-side state for someone who may not approve (State Patterns › Surface states lists "Not an administrator" for B6 and B6b only; Interaction Primitives "approver roles [OPEN: architecture]"). *Scenario:* a staff member without approval rights follows the Slack link and finds **Approve this repair** on B3. The mechanism is open, but the screen state isn't designed. *Fix:* define "You can't approve this. Samira or Cris can; we've told them." on B3 and B3-M, without Approve. Send decision notifications only to people who can decide.

- **[medium]** The delegation default and the effect of revoking it on approvals in flight are undefined (Key Flows › Flow 6 step 5; Surface → journey closure gap "Revoke delegation"; Authorisation & approvals "Delegation"). *Scenario:* the channels start ticked at onboarding, so does delegation too? Samira revokes delegation at 01:04, one minute after an on-behalf approval that hasn't run yet: does it still run? *Fix:* state that delegation starts off. Revoking it cancels any unused on-behalf approvals immediately, with words on screen and in the history. B6 shows the current scope and the last approval given on the customer's behalf.

- **[medium]** Sign-in through a Slack deep link teaches people to fall for phishing (Information Architecture › Slack notification "(signs in)"; Interaction Primitives "Notifications deep-link through sign-in"). *Scenario:* an attacker sends a lookalike "Nightshift proposes a repair that needs your approval. Open in Quornex portal" leading to a credential-harvesting page, and a half-awake owner signs in. *Fix:* each notification carries a short request code that the portal also shows. Add a line such as "If unsure, open the Quornex portal yourself; the request is waiting there." Keep this tied to A11.

- **[medium]** Names an attacker controls, and AI markup, can pass for labels (Voice and Tone › Exact labels "What this changes: your production (checkout on shop-eu-1)"; Key Flows › UJ-1 step 4 "restart checkout-api (its only replica)"; Component Patterns › Explanation block). *Scenario:* a deployment named `checkout-api-approved-by-orvex` reads naturally inside a prose sentence. AI text in the yellow block contains its own "Verified facts:" heading or "Orvex has approved this". [OPEN: architecture A6] owns the neutralising mechanism, but the spines put names inside prose. *Fix:* show identifiers as distinct tokens behind a fixed label ("Target: deployment `checkout-api`, namespace `shop`"), truncated and never turned into links. Render AI text as plain text with no headings, links or formatting. The UI, never the text, provides the block titles.

- **[medium]** The manual performer's notices, reassignment and identity are not designed (Key Flows › Flow 7 step 4 "Ready for Marc"; Open items A8; FR-52 "Who performs it"). *Scenario:* nothing reminds Marc when the window opens, so it lapses and everyone is asked again. If Marc is ill, there is no way to reassign. A hardware provider has no portal identity, so the customer presses **I've done it** for work they didn't do, and the record names them as the performer. *Fix:* notify the performer when all approvals are in and again when the window opens. Add **Reassign performer**: the approvers are told, and the customer is asked again if the performer's organisation changes. Record "Reported done by Cris (work done by the hardware provider)".

- **[medium]** Manual outcomes use three vocabularies and leave gaps (Voice and Tone › Exact labels "Manual repair progress" uses "Problem still there"; Component Patterns › Progress steps uses "Succeeded / Failed"; State Patterns › Repair status → FR-34 outcome; Surface states "next step not recorded"). *Scenario:* the check can't decide because the data is out of date, or the manual work makes things worse. Neither case has a status, an FR-34 outcome or a next step. After "Problem still there" it is unclear whether the approvals are used up. *Fix:* use one set of words: "Nightshift's check: problem gone / problem still there / can't tell yet (no data since …)". Map each to FR-34. After "still there" the problem stays open, the approvers are told and the next catalogue step is proposed. "Made things worse" follows FR-28.

- **[medium]** Changes to settings, mode, delegation and policy acceptance are missing from the history (Information Architecture › B4, A5; Component Patterns › Settings row "Changes are recorded and notified"). *Scenario:* the customer can't see who turned notifications off or widened delegation. Josh can't see when a customer switched to alert-only. *Fix:* B4 and A5 include settings events with the person and time: "Notifications turned off by Samira at 22:04".

- **[medium]** For double approvals, neither side sees the other's validity, and only the customer-first order is designed (State Patterns › Approval validity; Voice and Tone › Exact labels "Approvals still required", "After Approve"). *Scenario:* in A3 Josh doesn't see that Cris's approval runs out in 4 minutes, so it lapses and Cris is woken again. When Orvex approves first, the customer's screen has no wording for an Orvex approval that is already given or about to run out. *Fix:* "Approvals still required" lists each approval with its state and its own "valid until". A3 shows "Cris's approval runs out at 02:44 (in 12 min)", and the A1 queue ranks by it. Draft wording for the Orvex-first order.

### Low

- **[low]** "Valid until 02:44" overpromises, and repeated re-asks invite rubber-stamping (Voice and Tone › Exact labels "After Approve"). *Scenario:* a material change cancels the approval at 02:20, although the screen promised 02:44. Being asked again at 03:00 for Orvex's delay teaches people to approve without reading. *Fix:* "Valid until 02:44, or sooner if the situation changes." Every repeat request opens with what is different since last time.

- **[low]** Wording drift around "Orvex" and "checking" (Voice and Tone › Exact labels "Customer approved; waiting for Orvex review"; Key Flows › UJ-2 step 4 "He is told Orvex is checking it"; "Nightshift is checking"). *Scenario:* "Orvex is checking" tells Cris someone is already looking when nobody has seen it yet, and "review" and "technical approval" read as two different steps. *Fix:* always say "Waiting for Orvex technical approval". Use "checking" only for Nightshift's verification.

- **[low]** **Accept this change** sits beside **Read what changed** (Privacy & outbound channels › "Agreed management policy"). *Scenario:* an administrator accepts a change that widens permissions without opening it. *Fix:* put **Accept this change** inside the change detail, below what it widens.

- **[low]** A3 doesn't show that the tick matches the catalogue version (Key Flows › UJ-3 step 4). *Scenario:* Josh approves a ticked repair, assuming the 12 Oct tick covers restart-only-replica v3. The executor then blocks it because the tick needs renewing. *Fix:* "Ticked by Samira on 12 Oct for v3 (current)", or "Tick needs renewing: v4 changed the conditions".

- **[low]** The morning summary flattens who permitted what (Key Flows › UJ-4 step 3 "Authorised by you. Worked."). *Scenario:* a risky ticked rollback also had Orvex technical approval, but the summary credits only the customer, which weakens FR-34's "who permitted it". *Fix:* "Ticked by you on 12 Oct · Orvex technical approval by Josh at 02:20 · Worked."

- **[low]** The wording for a withdrawn request isn't drafted, including when the customer's approval was waiting (State Patterns › Surface states "Request withdrawn"). *Scenario:* Cris approved, the problem went away and the buttons vanished. He can't tell whether his approval was used. *Fix:* "The problem went away at 02:30. Your approval wasn't used and has been cancelled. Nothing was changed."
