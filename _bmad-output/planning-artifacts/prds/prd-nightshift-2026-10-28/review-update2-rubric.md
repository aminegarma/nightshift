Cross-references read: Glossary (Alert-only mode, Orvex technical approval), 3.2, FR-23, FR-24, FR-27, FR-28, FR-31, FR-46, FR-47, PRIV-8, SEC-7, SEC-8, SEC-9, NFR-3, NFR-5, UJ-5, and the UX `EXPERIENCE.md` where the PRD now defers to it. ## Overall verdict

The Update is well sourced and honest: every new rule carries its attribution, Section 10 items 18 and 22 are closed with the decision stated rather than just ticked off, and the required-heartbeat change carries its consequences through FR-6, NFR-4, PRIV-9 and the Glossary. The risk is in the authorisation model. Three changes create or expose gaps an architect or story writer would trip on: a group approval that contradicts the Glossary's single-use approval, an FR-51 acceptance gate whose deciding question ("within the agreed scope") has no owner, and a dangerous platform repair that one engineer can approve, plan-review and perform. Several new timing rules are also tied to events instead of bounds, so "done" is unclear. None of this blocks the UX, which is final. All of it matters for the architecture and stories.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

Decisions are stated as decisions and the superseded rules are named: FR-51 says it "replaces the rule of 2026-11-02"; FR-22 keeps the trail from "no timers" to per-entry validity; the memlog records that AI_LOG #27 is partly superseded. Section 10's triage now says outright that 18 and 22 "were decided in the UX work". The required heartbeat states its trade-off: a strict no-outbound deployment "cannot use managed Fleet", and NFR-4 now says what fails.

Two decisions are stated without the conflict they create being resolved. The group approval (FR-32) clashes with the Approval definition, and the FR-51 change moves the governance gate from "the customer accepts each version" to "Orvex's release applies unless it expands scope", but never says who decides that.

### Findings
- **high** Group approval contradicts the single-use approval (§ FR-32 last bullet; Glossary "Approval"; FR-22 bullet 2; SEC-9) — FR-32 now says "One Orvex approval may cover one problem group across clusters", while the Glossary still defines an approval as "single-use, tied to the exact repair, target, catalogue version and evidence shown" and FR-22 says "valid for that one time only". Neither says the group approval is an exception, how a per-entry approval validity (FR-22) works for a rollback that "runs in waves" across 300 clusters, or which targets the approval binds. The UX already has the answers ("targets are frozen at the click … clusters added later need a new approval", `EXPERIENCE.md` › Fleet-wide), but the PRD does not. "Problem group" is also used once and never defined. *Fix:* add to the Glossary "Approval" entry: "except a fleet-wide Orvex approval (FR-32), which binds a frozen set of clusters with the same problem group and is recorded and verified per cluster". In FR-32, state that targets are frozen at approval, later clusters need a new approval, and how validity applies across waves. Define "problem group".
- **high** FR-51 acceptance gate has no owner (§ FR-51 "Changes"; Glossary "Agreed management policy"; FR-47) — customer acceptance now applies only to "a change that expands permissions or adds production impact". Everything else "applies without fresh acceptance". Nothing says who decides whether a change is "within the scope the customer has already agreed", what that scope is made of (components, action kinds, risk levels?), or how the customer can dispute it. FR-21 has a dispute path for production classification; FR-51 has none. Because Orvex both writes the policy and, by default, classifies changes, a misclassified change bypasses the only customer gate. Two related tensions: (a) "adds production impact" can't happen, since Limits says the policy never holds a repair that can affect customer production, and the parenthetical admits this, so the clause either means something else or does nothing; (b) "does not let a cluster stay indefinitely on an old software or policy version" sits uneasily with FR-47, where routine updates run only under "the permitted update types and maintenance windows" the customer authorised. *Fix:* define the agreed scope, for example as a list of action types and components in the contract. Say who classifies a change as within scope (FR-39's two reviewers?), that the customer can read the classification and dispute it (mirroring FR-21), and what "expands permissions" covers. Either drop "or adds production impact" or define it. Add one sentence reconciling with FR-47 (the policy travels in the release but is installed under the maintenance policy's windows).
- **low** Pre-ticked channels as "explicit permission" without stated reasoning (§ PRIV-9 "At onboarding"; PRIV-8) — Fleet reports, notifications and the portal "start ticked" and "confirming the screen is the explicit permission", while external AI "starts with nothing selected". The difference is defensible (the managed service needs the first three; external AI is a third-party processor), but the PRD doesn't say so. The privacy review will ask why a pre-ticked box counts as explicit. *Fix:* add a clause, e.g. "these channels are needed to run the managed service; external AI is optional processing by a third party, so it starts unselected".

## Substance over theater — strong

The changed text is product-specific with no filler: exact status strings in FR-6 ("Heartbeat OK · Fleet reports not permitted: not included in fleet-wide correlation"), named onboarding defaults, freshness on every dashboard, a text message carrying the portal link. No findings.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The changes serve the PRD's thesis (no harm, nothing runs on silence, nothing leaves the cluster unseen). "A fresh approval cannot override" a failed check (FR-22) and "Switching to alert-only cancels pending execution authorisations" (FR-21) strengthen it. The required heartbeat moves the managed service towards Orvex's operability, and the PRD says so instead of hiding it. The one place coherence weakens is FR-51, which shifts control from the customer to Orvex. That is covered under Decision-readiness. No separate findings.

## Done-ness clarity — thin

Several new rules are clear and testable: onboarding explains "its purpose and its exact fields" (FR-6), freshness with a threshold pointer (FR-15), "read and answered on a phone" (FR-14), approval validity per catalogue entry (FR-22). But many of the new authorisation rules are tied to events or actors the PRD doesn't bound. An engineer could not write acceptance tests for them without going to the UX document.

### Findings
- **high** A dangerous platform repair can be one engineer from start to finish (§ FR-52 "Before the person performs it" and "Who performs it") — the change makes the platform-only case explicit: "an authorised Orvex engineer approves and reviews the recovery plan". "Who performs it" says "an Orvex engineer for OMK and Quornex". Nothing stops the same engineer approving, reviewing the plan and performing a dangerous repair. The earlier passive "the recovery plan is reviewed" at least left room for a second person. The addendum's "require two different people where two approvals are needed" doesn't apply, because only one approval is needed here. This is the class of gap the 2026-11-02 review rated critical ("manual dangerous repairs have no controls"). *Fix:* add "The performer is never the engineer who approved it" (or require a second reviewer for the recovery plan on platform-only dangerous repairs).
- **medium** Undo-a-rejection window is tied to an instant event (§ FR-50 last bullet) — "until Nightshift has notified the other approvers". Notification is normally immediate, so the window is effectively zero. Where no other approvers are concerned (a customer-only repair), the window never closes. *Fix:* state a bound, e.g. "for N seconds after rejecting, during which Nightshift holds the notification", and say what happens when no other approver exists.
- **medium** Withdrawing an approval is not guaranteed under disconnection (§ FR-22 "The person who gave an approval can withdraw it until the repair starts"; PRIV-9 "Without the portal"; SEC-9; NFR-5) — approvals are verified in the cluster (SEC-9) and "stay valid until their validity ends or the repair runs" when the portal is off. A withdrawal sent while the cluster is cut off or the portal is off cannot arrive, so the promise "until the repair starts" fails exactly when it matters. "The server orders decisions" names a component the PRD doesn't define (the portal? the executor?). *Fix:* "can withdraw it until the repair starts, if the withdrawal reaches the cluster first; the record shows a withdrawal that arrived too late". Say which component orders decisions, or leave that to the architecture and keep the rule as "decisions are ordered and the record keeps every step".
- **medium** A failed or inconclusive check has no next step, and two bullets pull against each other (§ FR-22 bullets 4 and 8; FR-52; FR-21 "in doubt") — bullet 4 says when conditions have materially changed "it does not run and Nightshift asks again". Bullet 8 says a failed check blocks the repair and "a fresh approval cannot override it". What the new request achieves is unclear. Does the repair wait and re-check when conditions change, go to Blocked, or escalate, and to whom? For a manual repair (FR-52), "the work does not start", but who is told? It is also unclear which check bullet 8 covers: FR-21's production-impact re-check routes doubt to the stricter row rather than blocking, while bullet 8 blocks on "inconclusive". *Fix:* one sentence of what follows: "the repair is marked Blocked, Nightshift keeps watching, and escalates as in FR-23; it may run later only if the check passes". Say that bullet 8 covers the catalogue safety preconditions, not the authorisation or classification checks.
- **medium** "Nobody answers" has no timer now that requests don't expire (§ FR-22 bullet 3; FR-23; FR-24; UJ-3 edge case) — FR-24's last-resort condition applies "only when nobody answers and failure is predicted within about 30 minutes", and FR-23 says "If they do not answer in time, the repair waits… alerts again". With "An unanswered request does not expire", nothing in the PRD marks the moment a request counts as unanswered, so the last-resort trigger and the re-alert are untestable. *Fix:* define "nobody answers" (e.g. "no decision within N minutes of the request, or before the predicted failure minus the last-resort threshold") and add it to Section 10 item 8 if the number is open.
- **medium** Manual repair time window and performer check: who states it, how it's recorded (§ FR-52 "Before the person performs it") — "The approvals cover a stated time window": stated by whom (the performer, as the UX assumes), and how does it relate to FR-22's per-entry approval validity? What if work is in progress when the window ends (the UX's answer, "finished or recovered under the reviewed recovery plan", is not in the PRD)? "The performer re-runs Nightshift's pre-start check": the customer or their hardware provider may be the performer, so how do they run it? FR-52's record bullet doesn't record the check result, so the rule can't be verified afterwards. *Fix:* state who proposes the window, that the window limits when work may start, and what happens at its end. Add "the result of the pre-start check" to what FR-52 records.
- **low** "Shown only when it is certain" is undefined (§ FR-14 "The request") — what makes a rollback certain? Probably that the catalogue entry has a tested rollback and its preconditions hold now. The request list also leaves out how long an approval will stay valid, which the UX shows ("Your approval is valid until 02:44"). *Fix:* "a rollback is shown only when the catalogue entry has a tested rollback whose preconditions hold". Add the approval's validity to the request contents.

## Scope honesty — adequate

Section 10 is updated honestly, and the addendum hands design details to their owners (UX open items O-1 to O-12 to the architecture). Two edges are left for the reader to infer.

### Findings
- **medium** "Strict no-outbound deployment" is a new offering with no scope line (§ FR-6 "Strict no-outbound"; Glossary "No outbound data"; NFR-4; 3.1/3.2; NFR-3; FR-46; SEC-6; FR-51) — "The customer is told this before choosing it" implies it can be chosen, but neither 3.1 nor 3.2 says whether the first version offers it. If it does, it collides with the rules for everything else: releases and catalogue downloads are outbound flows (addendum, adversarial H4), so NFR-3 ("the same tested release everywhere"), FR-46's rollout, SEC-6's fleet-wide switch-off and the agreed management policy (part of the managed-service contract) cannot reach it. The term also has three phrasings: "No outbound data", "strict no-outbound deployment" and "permits no outbound channel" (FR-15). *Fix:* add a line to 3.1 or 3.2: either "[NON-GOAL for MVP] strict no-outbound deployments" or "offered as an unmanaged deployment: releases and catalogue are installed by hand, no fleet switch-off, no management policy". Settle on one term in the Glossary.
- **low** Switching to alert-only leaves open requests and other permissions unaddressed (§ FR-21 first bullet; Glossary "Alert-only mode") — the bullet covers approvals given but not used, ticks and the policy. It doesn't cover open (unanswered) requests, which alert-only never asks for and which no longer expire, or last-resort permissions and delegations. *Fix:* "open requests are withdrawn; last-resort permissions and delegations stay recorded but do not run".

## Downstream usability — adequate

The changes keep the ID scheme stable (no new IDs) and cross-references resolve. Two changes lean on terms that exist only in the UX or nowhere.

### Findings
- **medium** "In-cluster dashboard" is now load-bearing but undefined, and FR-15's condition is out of date (§ FR-15 bullet 5; PRIV-9 "Without the portal" and "Without notifications as well") — PRIV-9 now sends open requests "only on the in-cluster dashboard" when the portal is off. FR-15 says dashboards are "reached through the Quornex portal", and the in-cluster dashboard appears only as a condition: "If the customer permits no outbound channel". Since the heartbeat is required, that condition now describes only a strict no-outbound deployment, not portal-off. The architecture has to host this dashboard and the UX has to design it, yet no requirement says who reaches it, from where, or whether requests can be answered there (approvals are portal-only, so they can't). *Fix:* add to FR-15: "Each cluster also serves an in-cluster dashboard, reachable from the customer's network, that shows problems and open requests; approvals cannot be given there". Change the condition to "If the portal channel is off (PRIV-9)". Add the term to the Glossary.
- **low** Record outcomes and UX statuses are not mapped, and FR-34 misses new events (§ FR-34; addendum "Final statuses") — the UX repair statuses add "Mitigated", "Problem still there" and "Work in progress", while FR-34's outcomes include "harmful" and "recovery failed", and nothing maps one to the other. FR-34 records "Rejected requests and expired approvals" but not withdrawn approvals, cancelled approvals (alert-only switch), undone rejections or withdrawn requests. FR-22's "the record keeps every step" covers them only implicitly. *Fix:* extend FR-34's "recorded too" list. Add one addendum line saying FR-34 outcomes are the record and UX statuses are the display, with a mapping owned by the UX.
- **low** Addendum UX bullet reads as both done and missing (§ addendum "From the review of the 2026-11-02 update" › UX) — "done in the UX run of 2026-11-03 … As handed over: the settings journey is still missing". A reader can't tell whether the settings journey and the nine stale decisions were resolved. *Fix:* "As handed over (now resolved in the UX run): …", or move the old text to a past-tense sentence.

## Shape fit — strong

Unchanged by this Update. The PRD stays a chain-top, multi-stakeholder product spec with named-protagonist UJs. The changed text keeps that shape and adds no unnecessary formality. No findings.
