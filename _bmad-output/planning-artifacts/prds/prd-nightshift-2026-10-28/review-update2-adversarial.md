# Adversarial review: PRD update of 2026-11-03

Reviewer stance: hostile SRE, security and privacy reviewer. Scope: the changes in `prd-update-2026-11-03.diff` and `addendum-update-2026-11-03.diff`, the change list `update-notes-2026-11-03.md`, and FR-51 "Changes" with Glossary "Agreed management policy" (edited directly earlier the same day). Each changed rule was checked against FR-6, FR-13 to FR-15, FR-21 to FR-24, FR-32, FR-34, FR-46, FR-47, FR-50 to FR-52, NFR-4, PRIV-2 to PRIV-9, SEC-7 to SEC-9, the Glossary and Section 10. Items already handed to the architecture are not findings unless the PRD rule itself is unsafe.

## Verdict

**Not ready to hand in until the critical and high findings are fixed.** None of them blocks the UX. The direction is sound: a required minimal heartbeat, fail-closed pre-start checks, versioned requests, withdraw and undo, and freshness on every dashboard. The new rules also add revocation and batching paths that the PRD does not tie to where enforcement actually happens, in the in-cluster executor (SEC-8, SEC-9). That creates three gaps:

- **Revocations look effective before they are.** A withdrawal, a switch to alert-only or turning off the portal can be shown as done while the cluster still holds a valid signed approval and runs the repair.
- **Some new approvals cover more than the approver saw.** These are the per-problem-group approval, the manual-repair time window and an approval that carries across a re-issued request.
- **Two consent changes promise more than they deliver.** Pre-ticked channel defaults are presented as "explicit" permission. The strict no-outbound deployment is named but never defined, so the PRD does not say how it gets releases, the fleet-wide switch-off (SEC-6) or the policy.

FR-51's new "within agreed scope" test has no owner and no definition, and it is decided by the party it benefits.

## Critical

- **[critical]** Withdrawals, the switch to alert-only and other revocations are promised at the server, but approvals are enforced in the cluster, so a repair can run after the customer was told it would not (FR-22 "withdraw … The server orders decisions"; FR-21 alert-only bullet; PRIV-9 "Without the portal"; SEC-9; SEC-8). *Scenario:* At 02:14 Cris approves a risky restart. The executor holds the signed approval, and its validity lasts 30 minutes (FR-22). At 02:16 the link between the portal and the cluster drops, and Cris taps **Withdraw**, or switches to alert-only. The portal server "orders" the decision, records "Approval withdrawn by Cris at 02:16" and shows it. The executor never receives the withdrawal, finds the approval valid and the preconditions passing, and restarts the only replica at 02:20. The same happens for a manual repair whose performer never presses "Start": "until the repair starts" is then judged by the portal, not by what the performer is doing. The customer's record says "withdrawn", the cluster's record says "executed under a valid approval", and SM-4 counts it as approved. *Fix:* State that a withdrawal, a cancellation caused by alert-only, an untick or a revoked delegation takes effect only when the in-cluster executor has received and acknowledged it. The portal shows "Withdrawal sent, not yet confirmed by the cluster" until then, and never "withdrawn". "Until the repair starts" is decided by the executor's own record of the start, or for a manual repair by the performer's start being recorded in Nightshift. If the executor cannot confirm, the person is told the repair may still run. Make SM-4 count any execution after a revocation the customer was shown as unapproved.

## High

- **[high]** Turning off the portal keeps approvals alive and takes away the only way to withdraw them or change settings (PRIV-9 "Without the portal"; FR-22 withdraw bullet; SEC-7). *Scenario:* A customer suspects the portal or an admin account has been compromised and turns off portal access, which is what SEC-9 anticipates. Any approval already given stays valid "until their validity ends or the repair runs", and the validity is set per entry with no upper limit. Withdrawal, which FR-22 promises "until the repair starts", is only possible in the portal, and PRIV-9 says open requests can then only be "seen" on the in-cluster dashboard. SEC-7 settings, including alert-only, the customer's one remaining stop, are also made in the portal, so the customer may be unable to stop anything. The justification "because the executor verifies them in the cluster" explains why keeping approvals is possible, not why it is the safe default. *Fix:* Turning off the portal cancels approvals given but not yet used, and records the cancellation, unless the customer explicitly chooses to keep them on the confirmation screen. Require an in-cluster path for authenticated customer admins to withdraw approvals, reject requests and change SEC-7 settings, at least alert-only and portal on or off, when the portal is off.

- **[high]** One Orvex approval for a "problem group" covers targets the approver never saw, which contradicts the Glossary's "single-use, tied to the exact repair, target, catalogue version and evidence shown" (FR-32 new bullet; Glossary "Approval"; FR-21; FR-22). *Scenario:* Josh approves the v2.4 rollback for the "v2.4 checkout-broker" group when it holds 288 clusters. Over the next hour 40 more clusters match the group. Their rollback is a risky platform repair that cannot affect customer production, so Orvex's approval is the only approval needed, and it is applied to all 328. The group also mixes clusters on two catalogue versions with different repairs, and the group approval's validity ends during wave 3 while waves 4 to 6 still run. Nothing says whether the batch can include dangerous (FR-52) or code-fix repairs. *Fix:* Define a problem group in the PRD: one repair at one catalogue version, with the set of clusters frozen at the moment of approval and shown with its count. Clusters added later need a new approval. Each cluster's executor checks that its own ID is in the signed set. Limit the batch to risky platform repairs and FR-47 rollbacks; dangerous and code-fix repairs are excluded. Add a stop rule between waves, such as a harmful or failed outcome in wave N stopping wave N+1 (as FR-46 does for releases). Say whether the validity covers the whole rollout or each wave, and when the remaining waves must be approved again. State that withdrawing the approval stops every wave that has not started.

- **[high]** A manual dangerous repair can now be controlled from start to finish by one Orvex engineer (FR-52 "Before the person performs it", "Who performs it"). *Scenario:* A dangerous OMK repair that "cannot affect customer production", such as changing a node's IP address on a live control-plane node, is a platform-row case. The FR-52 text lets one authorised Orvex engineer give the approval, review the recovery plan they will rely on, propose the time window (EXPERIENCE.md says "proposed by the performer") and perform the work, because "an Orvex engineer" performs OMK and Quornex repairs. Before this update the reviewer was unspecified, so a second person was at least possible. Now a single engineer covers the riskiest class of repair, while a new catalogue entry needs two reviewers (FR-39). The classification "cannot affect customer production" is exactly what the earlier review (C1) showed to be fragile. *Fix:* For every dangerous repair, the performer must be a different person from the Orvex engineer who gives technical approval and reviews the recovery plan. Record both names, and have the executor or portal refuse a performer who is also the approver. If Orvex wants one person to be able to do both at night, require a second engineer to approve, through the backup engineers SM-8 already assumes.

- **[high]** "Inconclusive blocks, and a fresh approval cannot override" leaves no way to fix a dangerous fault when Nightshift is blind, which pushes the work out of the record (FR-22 new bullet; FR-52; FR-7 limited-visibility mode; FR-20). *Scenario:* A node's network card fails, which is the dangerous repair the Glossary names. Because the node is unreachable, and the server is in limited-visibility mode without out-of-band management, Nightshift's pre-start check of the evidence is inconclusive by construction. The work "does not start", no approval can override that and no other path exists. The customer's hardware provider replaces the card anyway, since it is the customer's own hardware. That work never passes through Nightshift's record, which defeats "nothing done in secret" (FR-33), and SM-3 and SM-4 never see it. The same deadlock hits an urgent rollback during an incident in which Nightshift's own data is stale. *Fix:* Keep fail-closed for anything Nightshift executes. For manual repairs and for inconclusive results, which are different from failed checks, define an escalation path. The request becomes "Blocked: check inconclusive" with what is missing. A recorded override then needs stronger approvals: the customer personally, plus two Orvex engineers, one of whom is not the performer, with an explicit statement of what could not be checked. Say that physical work the customer does on their own hardware outside Nightshift is recorded as "performed outside Nightshift" when the customer reports it, not prevented.

- **[high]** The strict no-outbound deployment is named but undefined, so a cluster can run Orvex's policy repairs with no updates, no fleet-wide switch-off and no one watching (FR-6 "Strict no-outbound"; Glossary "No outbound data"; NFR-4; FR-46; FR-47; FR-51; SEC-6; SEC-9). *Scenario:* A customer chooses strict no-outbound. The PRD says only that this "cannot use managed Fleet" and that "Nightshift keeps working locally". Several things then go unanswered. Releases reach clusters through Fleet's controlled rollout (FR-46), and FR-51 says a cluster may not stay indefinitely on an old policy version. Security updates are required by contract (FR-47). SEC-6 lets Orvex switch off a harmful catalogue entry across the fleet, but Fleet cannot reach this cluster, so a catalogue entry later found to be harmful keeps running there under ticks and the agreed management policy, with nobody at Orvex able to see it. Nothing says whether this customer has the managed service at all, so whether the agreed management policy ("part of the managed-service contract") applies is also unknown. *Fix:* Define the strict no-outbound deployment as its own offering. Releases, catalogue entries and policy versions are delivered by a signed offline import that the customer runs, and the import includes SEC-6 switch-off lists. Agreed-management-policy repairs and Orvex-only approvals are unavailable, because Orvex cannot see or stop them. The cluster shows the customer that it is not watched externally and what release and switch-off list it is on. Onboarding says all of this before the customer chooses it. Alternatively, state that strict no-outbound is out of scope for the first version.

- **[high]** Pre-ticked channels confirmed with one click are presented as "explicit" permission (PRIV-9 lead and "At onboarding"; Section 10, item 22; Section 3.2 non-goal). *Scenario:* The onboarding screen arrives with Fleet reports, notifications (now including text messages through a telecoms provider) and the portal already ticked. An Orvex installer completing installation (FR-7), or a busy owner, clicks **Confirm**, and PRIV-9 declares that click "the explicit permission". That is opt-out consent: under GDPR case law (Planet49) pre-ticked boxes are not valid consent. It also contradicts PRIV-9's own heading and PRIV-8, which rightly starts with nothing selected. Diagnostic summaries then leave by default, and the customer's privacy claim ("only through the channels the customer permits", Vision) rests on a click they may not have made themselves. *Fix:* Either show each optional channel as an unticked choice with a recommended setting, or keep the defaults and stop calling the confirmation "explicit". In that case say plainly that these channels are on by default and can be turned off, and remove "explicitly" from PRIV-9 for them. In both cases, the confirmation must be made by an authenticated customer administrator (SEC-7), never by Orvex staff on the customer's behalf. It is recorded with who confirmed, when and the screen version, and every channel's fields are shown on the screen.

- **[high]** The FR-51 test "within the scope the customer has already agreed" versus "expands permissions or adds production impact" has no definition and no owner, and Orvex, which benefits, effectively decides it (FR-51 "Changes"; Glossary "Agreed management policy"; FR-22 tick versioning). *Scenario:* A release changes an existing policy entry "Restart the Quornex ingress sidecar", loosening its precondition from "when error rate > 5% for 10 min" to "when error rate > 1% for 2 min", and adds a new entry, "Rotate the Quornex internal certificate". Orvex classifies both as "within scope", because the contract scope is "routine platform repairs to OMK and Quornex". So both apply with only a notification, and the customer cannot stay on the old version. A ticked repair whose conditions change needs a fresh tick (FR-22), but a policy entry whose conditions loosen does not. The other branch, "adds production impact", needs acceptance, yet Limits says a production-affecting repair never enters the policy. That branch is therefore either empty or a route for such repairs into the policy once the customer clicks accept. *Fix:* Define the agreed scope as a list of action types and components that the customer can read. Any new action, any new target type and any loosened precondition, limit or frequency counts as expanding permissions by definition. Have this classification made by the two FR-39 reviewers and shown to the customer per change, with the dispute route FR-21 already gives for production classification. Delete "or adds production impact" (it cannot happen under Limits), or replace it with "would move an entry to the customer-production row, which removes it from the policy (FR-21)".

## Medium

- **[medium]** A heartbeat "accepted with the service" has no withdrawal path (PRIV-9 lead; FR-6; SEC-7; Section 3.2 non-goal). *Scenario:* A customer later wants to stop the heartbeat, for example after a data-protection review. SEC-7 lets admins change "outbound channel permissions", and the non-goal says Nightshift never sends through a channel that is not permitted, but the heartbeat is "required". Nothing says whether the customer can untick it, what happens if they block it at their firewall (Fleet just shows "No data since 02:10" and Orvex may treat it as an outage), or what happens to policy repairs, Orvex-only approvals and updates once Fleet cannot see the cluster. *Fix:* State that the heartbeat cannot be turned off in settings; stopping it means moving to the strict no-outbound offering through Orvex, with the consequences defined in the strict no-outbound finding above. State that a heartbeat blocked by the customer is shown to Orvex as "No data" and handled as an outage until Orvex confirms the change.

- **[medium]** Disabling only some of a new policy version's actions creates an untested combination for each customer (FR-51 "Changes", second sub-bullet; FR-17; FR-40; NFR-3). *Scenario:* Version 7 replaces policy action A with a safer action B, which needs acceptance, and removes A. A customer who has not accepted runs version 7 without B and without A: the repair disappears and the problem is escalated every night. Or the rest of version 7 assumes B ran first. The release was tested as a whole (FR-40), not as "version 7 minus B". *Fix:* State that a release's tests cover each combination of new actions that can be left disabled, or that an action being replaced stays active, at its old version, until the customer accepts its replacement. Record which policy actions are active per customer.

- **[medium]** Text messages are a new outbound medium bundled into the existing "notifications" channel. They train customers to approve from a texted link and can weaken critical alerting (FR-13 phone row; FR-14 Channels; PRIV-2; PRIV-3; PRIV-9; FR-24). *Scenario:* A customer permits "notifications" for Slack and, with that one tick, also permits texts through a telecoms gateway, where messages are stored by carriers and shown on lock screens. Critical texts carry a portal link, so an attacker who spoofs the sender ID sends "Checkout failing: approve the fix at quornex-portal.help" and harvests credentials. If the link itself signs the user in, a SIM swap gives the attacker approvals. A customer who picks "text instead of call" misses the 3 AM critical text, so "nobody answers" comes sooner and more risky repairs run under the last-resort condition. *Fix:* Make text messages a separately permitted sub-channel with its own allowed fields (PRIV-3). Require that the link carries no credential or approval token and always needs full sign-in, with step-up for approvals. Tell the customer in onboarding the portal domain that texts will use. When the customer chooses texts only, state in settings that this may let last-resort repairs run sooner, and keep a call when a last-resort repair is about to run.

- **[medium]** A re-issued request can still carry approvals given on its earlier version (FR-22 "re-issued if the evidence or conditions materially change"; Glossary "Approval … tied to … evidence shown"; addendum H9). *Scenario:* The repair needs the customer's approval and Orvex technical approval. Cris approves version 1 at 02:14, which says "Expected interruption: 30 s, one replica". At 02:20 the evidence changes and the request is re-issued as version 2, saying "two replicas affected". Josh approves version 2, and the executor finds two valid approvals for the same repair and target, so the repair runs with Cris's approval of evidence he never saw. *Fix:* State that re-issuing a request cancels every approval given on earlier versions and records the cancellation, and that the executor checks every approval names the same request version.

- **[medium]** Approval validity per catalogue entry has no default, no upper limit and no rule for a missing value, and Section 10 marks it "decided" with no value anywhere (FR-22 bullet 3; Glossary "Repair catalogue"; Section 10, item 18; FR-23; UJ-3 "in time"). *Scenario:* An entry is published without a validity value, or with "7 days". An approval given on Monday for a risky restart is still valid on Friday, after the portal has been turned off (see the portal-off finding). FR-23 and UJ-3 still say "if they do not answer in time" although requests no longer expire, so the escalation trigger is undefined. *Fix:* Set a PRD-level maximum, for example no longer than the time to impact and never more than a few hours for risky repairs. An entry with no value is invalid, so its approvals are refused. The value is reviewed by the two FR-39 reviewers. Reword FR-23 and UJ-3 to "if they do not answer within the escalation time", and add that escalation time to Section 10, item 8.

- **[medium]** A request that never expires is not closed when the problem is handled another way, so a late approval can act on a handled problem (FR-22 "An unanswered request does not expire"; FR-24; FR-21 alert-only; FR-50). *Scenario:* A risky restart request is open. At 03:40 the same repair runs under the last-resort condition, or a safe ticked repair resolves part of the problem, or the customer switches to alert-only. The request stays open, because only "the problem resolves" withdraws it. At 09:00 Samira approves it from her phone, the preconditions pass on a recurrence and the restart runs a second time. "Withdrawn if the problem resolves" also depends on the data being current: when monitoring goes stale, the problem can look resolved. *Fix:* State that a request is withdrawn and recorded when its repair or a different repair runs on the same target, when the mode changes to alert-only, or when the problem is handed to a manual repair. A request is never withdrawn as resolved on stale data (FR-15).

- **[medium]** The window for undoing a rejection depends on an event that can be instant or never happen, and nothing says what the undo brings back (FR-50 new bullet). *Scenario:* If notifications go out immediately, the window is effectively zero and the UX's **Undo reject** never works. If the other approver's channels are off (PRIV-9), they are never notified, so the rejection can be undone days later. If the undo restores earlier approvals given before the rejection, the repair can run at once on approvals given hours earlier, with no new check of what has changed. Undone rejections are not in FR-34's list of what is recorded. *Fix:* Give the undo a fixed short window, measured by the server and confirmed by the executor, for example 60 seconds or until the first other approver has seen the rejection, whichever comes first. State that an undo returns the request to "Waiting for approval" with every earlier approval cancelled. Record the undo (FR-34).

- **[medium]** Only "the person who gave an approval" can withdraw it, so the customer cannot pull back an approval made on their behalf (FR-22 withdraw bullet; FR-21 delegation; FR-50). *Scenario:* Lea from Orvex support approves a restart on Cris's behalf at 01:03. Cris sees the notice (FR-21) and wants to stop it, but cannot withdraw Lea's approval. Revoking the delegation does not, as written, cancel approvals already given under it. Whether Cris can still reject after Lea approved is unclear in the PRD; EXPERIENCE.md says yes. *Fix:* State that the customer can withdraw any approval given on their behalf, that revoking a delegation cancels its unused approvals, and that any required approver can reject until the repair starts (as EXPERIENCE.md already says).

- **[medium]** "Switching to alert-only cancels pending execution authorisations" leaves several cases unstated (FR-21 alert-only bullet; FR-24; FR-47; FR-52; Glossary "Alert-only mode"). *Scenario:* Several cases are unstated:
  - A repair already running when the switch arrives might be stopped halfway, finished or rolled back.
  - Open approval requests stay open, so someone can still approve them during alert-only.
  - Last-resort permissions and FR-47 maintenance and rollback policies may or may not "stay recorded".
  - Approvals for a manual dangerous repair, which Nightshift does not execute, may or may not be cancelled.
  - When the customer switches back weeks later, every tick, policy entry and last-resort permission starts running again without warning.

  *Fix:* State the following:
  - A repair in progress finishes or recovers under its verification and rollback, and is never left halfway (FR-31).
  - Open requests are withdrawn.
  - Last-resort permissions stay recorded and do not run.
  - FR-47 policies are unaffected, as the Glossary says.
  - Manual-repair approvals are cancelled.
  - Switching back shows the customer the ticks, policy and last-resort permissions that will become active, and needs their confirmation.

- **[medium]** The manual-repair time window has no owner and no upper limit, and the PRD does not cover the window ending mid-work. It also runs alongside FR-22's per-entry validity (FR-52 "Before the person performs it"; FR-22 bullet 3). *Scenario:* The performer proposes "this week". The approvers approve, and the approval stays usable for days, while FR-22 says the entry's own validity applies too, so the two rules conflict. A network change is halfway done when the window closes, and "outside it, the approvers are asked again" read literally means stopping a dangerous repair partway through to ask. EXPERIENCE.md decided "finished or recovered, never abandoned", but the PRD does not say so. The pre-start check is run by the performer, who may be the customer or their hardware provider, and "the work does not start" cannot be enforced on a person with physical access. It is also a "check of the evidence", whereas FR-22 refers to a "safety check". *Fix:* State that the window limits when work may start, is proposed by the performer and accepted by the approvers, and is no longer than the entry's approval validity (FR-22). Work in progress when the window ends is finished or recovered under the reviewed plan and recorded. The pre-start check covers the entry's safety preconditions as well as the evidence. For performers outside Orvex, it is a recorded gate: starting without a passing check is recorded as "started against a failed check" and counted in SM-4.

- **[medium]** FR-22 now gives two different outcomes for the same failed check (FR-22 bullet 4 "If the repair or its conditions have materially changed, it does not run and Nightshift asks again" versus new bullet "If a pre-start safety check fails or is inconclusive … a fresh approval cannot override it"). *Scenario:* A precondition such as "the healthy replicas can carry the traffic" no longer holds. Under bullet 4 this is a material change of conditions, so Nightshift asks again, and a new approval would then let the repair run. Under the new bullet a fresh approval cannot override it. A tester cannot write one expected result, and an implementer may treat the re-ask as the override. *Fix:* Separate the two cases. When a safety precondition fails or the result is inconclusive, the request is blocked with no override and is re-checked automatically as data changes. When the evidence or target changes but the preconditions still pass, the request is re-issued and needs new approvals.

## Low

- **[low]** "Never shows stale data as healthy" can be defeated by a frozen server and does not cover clusters whose Fleet reports are off (FR-15 new bullet; FR-6 "Heartbeat OK · Fleet reports not permitted"). *Scenario:* The in-cluster dashboard's back end hangs and keeps serving "Updated 40 s ago". On the Fleet grid, a tile showing "Heartbeat OK" next to green "No open problem" tiles reads as healthy, though Fleet knows nothing about that cluster's problems. *Fix:* Compute freshness from the data's own timestamp where it is displayed, not as a label the server declares. State that a cluster whose Fleet reports are off is never shown as "no open problem".

- **[low]** "The request can be read and answered on a phone" can be read as answering by phone call or by text reply, which contradicts "only notify" (FR-14 new bullet; FR-13). *Fix:* "The portal request can be read and answered in a phone's web browser; a call or text message never carries a decision."

- **[low]** "A rollback is shown only when it is certain" cannot be tested (FR-14 recovery-plan sentence). *Fix:* "A rollback is shown only when the catalogue entry defines a tested rollback for this target; otherwise the request says the repair cannot be undone."

- **[low]** FR-34's list of what is recorded misses the new decision types (FR-34; FR-22; FR-50; FR-21). *Scenario:* Withdrawn approvals, undone rejections, approvals cancelled by alert-only or by a re-issued request, and re-issued or withdrawn requests are "recorded" only in passing or not at all, so SM-4 reconciliation cannot rebuild who decided what. *Fix:* Add them to FR-34's list.

- **[low]** NFR-4 says Fleet "notices the silence", but no rule says who is told or how fast (NFR-4; FR-6; Section 10, item 8). *Fix:* "…and the on-duty engineer is alerted within the threshold of Section 10, item 8."

- **[low]** The required heartbeat's "degraded" status tells Orvex a cluster has a problem even when the customer has refused Fleet reports (FR-6 heartbeat fields; PRIV-9 "Without Fleet reports … receives no diagnostic reports"). *Fix:* Define "degraded" as the Nightshift agent's own health only, never the cluster's or the app's, and say so on the onboarding screen.

- **[low]** Clusters whose Fleet reports are off are left out of the consolidated alert and the group rollback for a fleet-wide bad update, but they still received the update (FR-32; PRIV-9). *Fix:* State that Fleet's update controller flags every cluster on the bad release, including those whose reports are off, so they are handled individually and are not forgotten.
