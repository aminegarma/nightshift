# Prose polish: PRD Nightshift

**Read of purpose and audience:** this document exists to help Orvex's judge (a technical founder) assess what Nightshift must do and why, and to give the UX and architecture authors an unambiguous set of requirements, terms and constraints to design against.

**Pass run:** prose only (the structure pass did not run; no structural moves proposed).

**Style and voice to preserve:** plain, short declarative sentences aimed at non-specialists; informal "yes" for an approval ("the customer's yes", "the Nightshift team's yes"); "tick/ticked" for advance permission; British spelling; no serial comma; bracketed source tags on almost every statement; "[OPEN]", "[To test]" and "[DESIGN ASSUMPTION ...]" markers. None of the rows below changes a rule, number, threshold or decision. Uncertain fixes are phrased "Consider: ...?".

## Term inconsistencies (summary)

- Orvex technical approval / Orvex's approval / Orvex approval / the Nightshift team's yes / Nightshift-team yes.
- Approval (at the time) used for advance permission: Customer production, UJ-4.
- Risk levels (safe, risky) applied to problems instead of repairs: FR-12, FR-13, FR-16, SM-8.
- Fault vs failure: FR-1, FR-8 ("disk ... failures", "sudden failures").
- Problem vs issue: FR-1.
- Alert vs call: UJ-5, FR-14 ("a phone call only alerts").
- Fix request vs approval request: §2, FR-12.
- Last-resort condition vs last-resort option: FR-24, SEC-7.
- Policy enabled vs authorised: maintenance vs rollback policy.
- On duty vs on-call: FR-32 example.
- First version vs version 1: COST-4.
- Fleet update controller vs Independent update controller: FR-32.
- "the catalogue" / "catalogue of problems" where the repair catalogue is meant: Advance permission, FR-21.
