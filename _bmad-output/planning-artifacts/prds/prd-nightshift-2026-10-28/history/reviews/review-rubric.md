# PRD Quality Review — Nightshift

*Reviewed 2026-10-30 against `prd-validation-checklist.md`. Inputs: `prd.md` (669 lines), `addendum.md`. Source tags such as "(Amine)" and "[AI]" are treated as deliberate traceability, and prose polish is out of scope.*

## Overall verdict

The safety core is decision-grade and traceable. It covers the two-catalogue split, the risk level × approval table (FR-20), prohibited actions, compromised-agent containment (SEC-2) and a real Non-Goals list, and an architect or grader can act on it. The main risks are elsewhere. The PRD never sets its dual-approval rule for risky repairs against its own thesis that Nightshift "really replaces engineers", and the fourth proof point has no comparator. Done-ness is thin because the PRD sets no timing bounds at all and gives FR-1 (detection) no testable scope. The journeys also under-serve the primary user, which matters because UX wireframes are the next step.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

Most decisions are stated as decisions, and several are crisp. Examples: "Dangerous | No" in the FR-20 tick column, "No answer is never a yes" (Glossary, Approval), FR-21 "There are no timers to manage", and PRIV-1 "even between clusters of the same customer". The Open Questions (§10) are open in fact. Each says who must answer it, and the "partly answered" notes are honest. Trade-offs are another matter: the PRD states what it chose but rarely what that choice costs. The biggest gap is the interaction between the approval model and the staffing/cost thesis. Two smaller trade-offs (PRIV-1, last-resort) are accepted without naming what they give up.

### Findings
- **[high]** Dual approval vs "replaces engineers" is never confronted (§3.5 FR-20, FR-22; §8 SM-8, SM-9; Glossary "Risky repair") — FR-20 requires "The Nightshift team's yes each time it is about to run" for every risky repair, even a ticked one. The Glossary's own risky example is "restarting one replica", which is likely among the most common Kubernetes remediations. SM-8 still rests on "most problems are not risky or dangerous" and on a 90% automatic share. SM-8's own arithmetic shows the estimate collapses at 60% ("about 75 hours a night"). FR-22 also staffs only "One Nightshift engineer ... each night". So the PRD's central safety choice and its central business claim pull against each other, and the document doesn't say so. *Fix:* add a short trade-off note in 3.5 or §8. It should estimate what share of catalogue repairs are risky, say what happens to SM-8 if that share is high, and name the levers: reclassifying repairs as safe after field evidence, Orvex approving in advance per catalogue entry rather than per run, or more night staff. Then link SM-9's target to that decision.
- **[medium]** PRIV-1's cost is not named (§4.1 PRIV-1, PRIV-4; §10 Q3) — "Private data stays in its cluster ... even between clusters of the same customer". This rules out cross-cluster root-cause analysis on raw logs for a single customer, and the management agent's "investigations" (FR-6, PRIV-4) are limited to sanitised data. The PRD records this as "Amine's design decision" but never says what diagnostic capability is lost. *Fix:* add one sentence naming the loss, for example "a fault visible only by correlating raw logs across two clusters of the same customer may be reported with uncertainty instead of explained". Also say whether the customer could opt in to relax the rule.
- **[medium]** The last-resort condition quietly removes the Orvex technical check (§3.5 FR-23; Glossary "Orvex technical approval") — The Glossary justifies Orvex's yes "so the customer cannot damage their own app". FR-23 then lets "Orvex's permission given in advance" replace that yes for risky repairs exactly when nobody answers, including the single on-duty engineer. That may be the right call, but it is accepted silently. It is also unclear who at Orvex gives that advance permission, and whether it is given per customer, per repair or per catalogue entry. *Fix:* state the trade-off (time-to-repair vs Orvex's per-run check) and define the advance-permission mechanism in one line.
- **[low]** An undecided item sits inside an FR (§3.5 FR-22) — "[Possible feature, Amine] For dangerous cases, Nightshift calls a member of the Orvex Nightshift team." Downstream readers can't tell whether this is in v1. *Fix:* decide it or move it to §10.

## Substance over theater — adequate

The Vision is not theater. Sovereign cloud for small businesses without infrastructure teams, 10,000 clusters, and "Raw data and secrets never leave the cluster" could not be swapped into another ops-agent PRD. There are three users, each of whom drives decisions: the non-expert owner drives FR-11 and NFR-8, the Nightshift team drives FR-20 and SM-8, and Orvex support drives the delegation rules. The counter-metrics (SM-C1 to C3) are specific, not ritual. The furniture is mostly repetition: several requirements restate one another, and some restatements have already drifted apart.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Duplicated requirements, already diverging (§3.4 FR-18/FR-25, FR-19/FR-35, FR-38/FR-44, FR-31 vs §3.8 description, FR-34, FR-45, FR-47/COST-4, PRIV-5/FR-41, NFR-8/FR-11) — FR-18 ranks by "the lowest-risk repair expected to work", while FR-25 ranks by "safety, estimated success rate and potential impact". FR-38 says catalogue entries ship "through a controlled release", while FR-44 adds a canary ("a small group of clusters first"). FR-45 and FR-34 add no requirement of their own; they only aggregate cross-references. Duplicates will be extracted twice downstream and will drift further. *Fix:* keep one canonical FR per rule (for example, merge FR-18 into FR-25 and FR-38 into FR-44). Have the others cross-reference it, and turn FR-34 and FR-45 into one-line pointers or delete them.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

There is a clear thesis: the four proofs in §1 (detect, explain honestly, repair without harm, replace engineers at lower cost). Most features trace to it. Primary SMs map to proofs 1–3, and counter-metrics balance SM-6, SM-9 and SM-10. Two parts don't hold together. Proof 4 can't be evaluated as written, and the MVP scope is "everything", so it is not derived from the thesis.

### Findings
- **[high]** Proof 4 has no comparator (§1 bullet 4; §4.3 COST-5; §8 SM-8, SM-10; §10 Q5) — The Vision promises to show "whether it costs Orvex less or more than they would". COST-5 compares total operating cost "with the engineers Nightshift replaces", but no baseline headcount or cost of that counterfactual DevOps team appears anywhere. SM-8 counts only Nightshift engineers, SM-10's target is "as low as realistically possible", and Q5 asks what cover Orvex has today, not what it would need without Nightshift. As written, the fourth proof cannot come out either way. *Fix:* add an SM or COST item for the counterfactual, such as engineers per N clusters without Nightshift, with a source or estimate tagged to test. State the comparison rule, for example "Nightshift wins if total operating cost per cluster < counterfactual cost per cluster at 10,000 clusters".
- **[medium]** Scope is everything, with no order within it (§9.1) — "Everything in Sections 3 to 5 ... Nothing has been deferred". Danny asked for this, so it is honest. But experimental code fixes (3.10) and the shared-GPU option don't serve any of the four proofs, and the PRD gives downstream UX and architecture no ranking for where to go deep before the 10–14 November hand-in. *Fix:* keep the scope and add a priority tier, for example "proof-critical: 3.1–3.8; experiment: 3.10; option study: Section 5 shared GPU".
- **[medium]** Detection proof only half measured (§1 bullet 1; §8 SM-1, SM-C1; FR-1, FR-3) — SM-1 covers only "slowly developing problems". Sudden failures, which FR-8 says are "detected (3.1), not predicted", have no metric or time bound, and SM-C1 has no target. *Fix:* add a detection-latency SM for sudden failures, or give SM-C1 a target.

## Done-ness clarity — thin

The safety rules are testable: FR-11's five required message parts, FR-20's table, FR-21, FR-28's worked example, FR-29's three consequences, FR-32's "target is 0" and FR-33. The perception side is the problem, and that is where architecture leans hardest. It has no time bounds anywhere, its core FR has no consequences, and its thresholds are undefined. Several failure-path FRs say what starts but not what ends.

### Findings
- **[high]** No timing bounds anywhere in the PRD (FR-2 "[OPEN] How long a heartbeat may be missing"; NFR-7 "[OPEN] Numeric targets"; FR-22 "If they do not answer in time"; FR-10 "changes substantially", "stays stable"; FR-14 no response window; FR-30 "stops the update immediately") — Architecture can't size scrape intervals, heartbeat periods, escalation timers or watchdog thresholds. Engineers can't write a pass/fail test for "noticed" or "in time". Q8 lists this as open, but NFR-7 offers not even a proposed figure. *Fix:* propose starting numbers tagged "to test", the way the PRD already does for the 30-minute last-resort threshold. Examples: heartbeat missing after N s, alert within M min of detection, on-duty engineer re-alert after X min, call after Y min.
- **[high]** FR-1 is untestable and its scope floats (§3.1 FR-1; Glossary "Supported problem", "Incident catalogue", "Repair catalogue"; §11 "Failure catalogue") — FR-1 says Nightshift "detects disk, network-card and power-supply failures, Layer 2/3 networking problems, misconfigurations and Kubernetes issues" with no consequences. "Misconfigurations and Kubernetes issues" is unbounded. The Glossary ties "supported" to catalogue entries. §11 then defers "the 20 most important failures" to a separate failure catalogue, but the PRD never says that deliverable seeds the incident and repair catalogues. *Fix:* give FR-1 a consequence: "every problem in the v1 failure catalogue (§11) is detected on each platform it is tagged for, verified by failure injection". Then add one line in the Glossary or §11 linking the failure catalogue to the two catalogues.
- **[medium]** FR-9 has factors but no rule (§3.2 FR-9) — Four factors are listed, with "actionability as the final requirement", but there is no rule for combining them and no thresholds, such as a minimum confidence or a time-to-impact window per criticality. Whether a prediction alerts can't be tested. *Fix:* give a decision rule or a small matrix (criticality × time-to-impact → alert/dashboard), marked to tune.
- **[medium]** FR-21 "asks again" is ambiguous (§3.5 FR-21) — "If something has changed, it does not run and Nightshift asks again." When a repair's catalogue safety preconditions fail, it is unclear whom Nightshift asks, and for what. Asking a person to approve a repair whose safety preconditions have failed would contradict FR-17/FR-18. *Fix:* say what happens instead, for example "re-evaluates; proposes the next eligible repair or escalates under FR-19".
- **[medium]** Failure paths that start but never end (FR-4 "opens a **monitoring failure case**"; FR-6 Fleet liveness; FR-27 "stops further automatic repairs on that cluster") — The PRD doesn't say who is told about a monitoring failure case, what Fleet does when an agent's "I'm alive" stops, or how and by whom automatic repairs are re-enabled after FR-27. *Fix:* add one consequence to each: recipient plus notification level, and the exit condition.
- **[medium]** FR-30 lacks recognition and scope criteria (§3.7 FR-30) — The PRD doesn't say how Fleet decides a problem is "the same problem" (cluster count, time window). It also doesn't say which "update" Fleet stops: a Nightshift release (FR-44), an OMK or Quornex update, or a customer app deploy. Nor does it say what each affected customer sees meanwhile. *Fix:* define the correlation trigger, list which update channels Fleet controls, and add the customer-side notification.
- **[medium]** Privacy controls are written as intentions (§4.1 PRIV-2 "approved fields", PRIV-3 "If needed, a further test runs") — The PRD doesn't say who approves the field list or how it is versioned, and "If needed" makes the outbound check optional with no trigger. The privacy and security review deliverable (§11) will need both. *Fix:* name the owner of the field allowlist (Orvex, via the tested release?), and make PRIV-3 either always-on or triggered by a stated condition.
- **[low]** FR-26 is hard to evaluate against FR-20 (§3.6 FR-26) — "moves to the next repair only if that repair is safe; otherwise it asks for approval from the Nightshift team". It then adds that an unticked repair also needs the customer's yes, and doesn't cover alert-only mode. *Fix:* restate it as "the next repair follows FR-20 exactly as if first; a previous approval never carries over".
- **[low]** Cost and tuning FRs have no measurable target (FR-39 "limits Orvex sets"; FR-47 "Cost targets are in Section 8", which has none; FR-43 metrics with no keep/drop threshold) — *Fix:* for FR-43, state the decision rule (for example "keep if net cost savings > 0 and fix success rate ≥ X"). For FR-47, point to the cost-model deliverable instead of §8.

## Scope honesty — adequate

§9.2 is a real Non-Goals list. It is phrased as hard rules with sources, and it pre-empts the riskiest silent assumptions: no improvised scripts, no silence-as-consent, no direct contact with the hardware provider, no provider takeover on EKS. Open items are visible as [OPEN], [AI], "to validate" and "DESIGN ASSUMPTION" markers and are mostly gathered in §10. Their density (about 17 open questions and roughly 10 inline flags) is reasonable for a design-challenge PRD that is not a build green-light. What's missing is a set of operational omissions a designer would fill in silently.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Silent omissions UX and architecture will assume (whole PRD) — The PRD never states:
  - **Daytime coverage.** The product is "Nightshift", and staffing is described only "each night" (FR-22, SM-8).
  - **Approver identity and roles at the customer.** Who may tick or approve, whether there are several users, and how a Slack yes is authenticated. SEC-2 says approvals cannot be forged, but only from the agent side.
  - **Retention of records.** FR-31–34 hold records with no stated lifetime, which also affects cost.
  - **Defaults.** Whether alert-only or approval-enabled is the default mode, what the default checklist is, and the default read scope.

  *Fix:* add one line for each, as a decision or as an [OPEN] item in §10.
- **[medium]** A human-access exception to code privacy is not listed with the privacy constraints (§3.10 FR-42 step 4; §4.1 PRIV-5) — FR-42 lets "an Orvex engineer ... securely access the test environment to inspect the code changes", while PRIV-5 says only "Customer code stays in the cluster". Human viewing of customer source is a privacy exception that the §11 privacy review must cover, but §4 doesn't record it. *Fix:* add it to PRIV-5 as a named, customer-authorised exception that is recorded under FR-31.
- **[low]** No Assumptions Index (whole PRD) — Assumptions are spread across "[DESIGN ASSUMPTION unless Danny confirms]" (Glossary Quornex Fleet; §3.7 single-cluster), SM-7 "(assumption; Danny's number still open)", FR-6 "[AI, to confirm]", the addendum's "AI reading, unconfirmed", and many "Amine's estimate, to test" notes. §10 "To test" covers only some of them. *Fix:* add a short indexed list at the end of §10.

## Downstream usability — adequate

A glossary is present and does real work: it fixes Fault/Failure/Incident/Problem, the three risk levels and the two modes. FR, NFR, SM, UJ, PRIV, SEC and COST IDs are contiguous and unique, and most cross-references resolve. §11 is a good addition for a chain-top PRD. The weak spots, given that UX wireframes come next, are that the journeys skip the primary user's everyday work, that some requirements have no IDs, and that UI surfaces and risk vocabulary drift.

### Findings
- **[high]** The primary user's everyday work has no journey (§6 Users, UJ-1–UJ-4) — The PRD names the Orvex Nightshift team as "Primary", but its one journey (UJ-2) covers the rare fleet-wide case. The common primary-user loop has none: approving a single risky repair under FR-20, which per SM-9 drives staffing. Also without a journey: reviewing a fleet lesson (FR-37), triaging a harmful repair (SM-3), working the grouped 100-cluster dashboards (FR-15), and handling a monitoring failure case (FR-4). Orvex support (delegated approval) has no journey at all. Three of four journeys are customer-side. Wireframes built from these journeys will under-design the Orvex console. *Fix:* add UJ-5, "the on-duty engineer approves one risky repair at 3 AM", and optionally a support-delegation journey.
- **[medium]** The journeys bypass the dual approval they should exercise (UJ-3, UJ-4 vs FR-20) — In UJ-4 the owner approves and "Nightshift applies the fix". A memory-creep fix is the Glossary's risky example, which needs the Nightshift team's yes too. UJ-3's pre-ticked rollback likewise omits Orvex's per-run yes if the rollback is risky, which UJ-1's edge case says it may be. UX will wireframe a single-approval flow. *Fix:* state each journey's repair risk level and show the second approval where it applies.
- **[medium]** Some requirements have no IDs (§3.4 "Hardware faults: mitigate, report uncertainty, escalate"; §3.7 "Coordination and local operation") — These carry real requirements, including the closing rule "stays open until the customer confirms the hardware is fixed", but downstream stories can't cite them. *Fix:* number them (FR-19a/b/c or new FRs) and keep the source tags.
- **[medium]** Risk vocabulary drifts from repairs to problems (FR-12 "not paged for safe problems"; FR-13 "A problem becomes risky"; FR-16 "For risky problems") — The Glossary defines risk levels only for repairs ("every repair in the repair catalogue has one of three risk levels"), and a problem may have repairs at several levels. Notification and routing rules therefore have no defined trigger. *Fix:* say "problems whose candidate repair is risky", or use FR-9's "criticality" for problems.
- **[medium]** UI surfaces are named but not defined (FR-14 "a Nightshift monitoring page"; FR-15 dashboards; FR-31 "where the customer can see it"; Glossary "a checklist in the settings"; §4.1 PRIV-6 "a record ... which the customer can inspect") — Settings are scattered across the Glossary, FR-20, FR-23, FR-20's "Reading is not changing" and PRIV-2's "maximum privacy". They cover mode, the checklist, the last-resort tick, delegation to support, read scope, app opt-in and privacy level. *Fix:* add a one-paragraph inventory of customer-facing surfaces and settings, so the wireframes have a screen list.
- **[low]** Protagonist naming (UJ-1 "Amine"; UJ-4 "the owner of an online shop") — UJ-1's protagonist has the same name as the "(Amine)" source tag used throughout, which will confuse extraction. UJ-4's protagonist is unnamed. *Fix:* rename UJ-1's protagonist and name UJ-4's.

## Shape fit — adequate

The product is multi-stakeholder B2B (non-expert customers plus Orvex operators) with real UX and a fleet back-end, so a capability spec with a handful of journeys is the right shape and the PRD uses it. The PRD isn't over-formalised: four UJs is light rather than heavy for this shape. The one misfit is that the Glossary works as a second requirements section.

### Findings
- **[medium]** The Glossary carries requirements (§2 "Repair catalogue", "Approval", "Advance permission", "Last-resort condition", "Disk cleanup", "Risk level") — The "Repair catalogue" entry is a paragraph of lifecycle rules. "Approval" restates FR-22 behaviour. "Risk level" holds a rule ("customers cannot change it") under an "Example" label. "Disk cleanup" is a deletion policy. Rules in the Glossary have no IDs and duplicate §3, so story extraction will miss or double them. *Fix:* cut entries back to definitions and move the rules into FRs. The catalogue lifecycle already exists as FR-35.

## Mechanical notes

- **ID continuity:** FR-1–47, NFR-1–8, SM-1–10 and SM-C1–C3, UJ-1–4, PRIV-1–6, SEC-1–4 and COST-1–6 are all contiguous with no duplicates. §10 numbering goes 7 (struck), 7a, 7b, 8…; this is acceptable, but renumbering before hand-in would be cleaner.
- **Broken or unresolved references:**
  - FR-36 cites "requirements checklist, assumption A2", which is outside the PRD.
  - FR-47 says "Cost targets are in Section 8", but §8 SM-10 has none, and §4.3 says "the numbers themselves belong in the separate cost-model deliverable".
  - UJ-2 cites "danny-qa follow-up #1". This is acceptable as traceability but doesn't resolve inside the PRD.
- **Undefined terms used as if defined:** "monitoring failure case" (FR-4), "repair coordinator" (FR-28), "Nightshift monitoring page" (FR-14), "Nightshift monitoring service" (FR-2), "morning summary", "fleet lesson" (FR-37), "failure catalogue" (§11; this also clashes with Glossary "Failure" = a service failing).
- **Glossary drift:**
  - "Orvex Nightshift team" / "Nightshift team" / "Nightshift engineer" / "on-duty engineer" / "Nightshift team engineer".
  - "Quornex Fleet" / "Fleet".
  - "ticked" / "advance permission" / "settings checklist" / "checklist of ticked repairs".
  - "update" (FR-30) vs "release" (FR-44).
  - "incident reviews" (SM-2) vs the Glossary's narrow "Incident".
  - "Incident catalogue" holds faults and failures as well as incidents.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** the PRD has no `[ASSUMPTION]` tags and no index (see Scope honesty). It uses equivalent markers instead.
- **`[NOTE FOR PM]`:** none present. [OPEN] and §10 serve the same role.
- **Front matter:** `updated: 2026-10-29`, but §4–§11 are dated 2026-10-30.
- **Glossary "Customer hardware":** the entry opens with a qualifier ("possibly badly configured") before the definition.
- **Required sections for the stakes:** Vision, Glossary, Features, Constraints, NFRs, SMs with counter-metrics, Scope/Non-Goals, Open Questions and downstream requirements are all present. Only an Assumptions Index is missing.
- **Addendum:** consistent with the PRD. Its "Where Quornex Fleet runs" note bears on the Glossary's Quornex Fleet DESIGN ASSUMPTION and could be cross-referenced there.
